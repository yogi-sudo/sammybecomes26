Chapter one

And then came the boy drama

In which the editor is ignored one hundred times

Istill remember the phase when you went from my chill, carefree girl to “I think I'm falling in love” girl. And of course, it had to be that one. You know I hate him.

I told you not to talk to him approximately one hundred times, probably more, and every single time you somehow found a reason to talk to him again.

I watched you go from excited, happy and completely in love to confused, hurt and crying. I watched the arguments, the misunderstandings, the breaking up, the getting back together, and all the “this is the last time” conversations.

And honestly, I hated seeing you hurt. There were so many moments when I wanted to shake you and say, choddye yr please vayo aba.

But there were also moments when I just sat there and listened, because sometimes you don't need someone to say I told you so. You just need your person to stay.

“And I stayed. Through every stupid decision, every heartbreak, every ‘I'm done with him’ and every ‘okay but…’.”

Chapter two

The criminal era

Statute of limitations: expired, probably

And then there was our criminal era. Sneaking out of your house just to go clubbing with me, like we were planning some international mission.

The panic before leaving. The checking if anyone was awake. The excuses. The “call me if they ask where I am.” The coming back home and pretending absolutely nothing happened.

And somehow, we thought we were being extremely smart.

Then came your birthday, and obviously we couldn't just celebrate normally. We literally snuck out of your house together on your birthday.

“I genuinely don't know how we survived some of the things we did. But those are the stories we'll still be laughing about when we're old.”

Chapter three

We have seen everything

Every version, on the record

We've seen each other at our absolute best. And at our absolute worst. The confident version, the emotional version, the confused version, the stubborn version, the dramatic version, the “I don't care” version when we very obviously cared.

We've seen each other cry. We've seen each other make terrible decisions. We've watched each other fall in love with the wrong people. We've given advice that was completely ignored.

We've had misunderstandings, mood swings, random fights, stupid arguments and days where life just felt heavy.

But somehow, through every version of ourselves, we never stopped choosing each other. That's probably my favourite thing about us. We don't love each other only when things are easy. We've loved each other through the messy parts too.

Chapter four

My birthday reminder to you

Read this part twice

If someone had told me in 2020 that the random girl I met in college would become one of the most important people in my life, I probably wouldn't have believed them. Because look at us now.

From seven people in a college group to somehow becoming just the two of us who are still here. From random café plans to actual trips. From talking about boys to talking about careers, money, future, life and everything in between.

From being two stupid college girls to slowly becoming adults who are still figuring everything out. We've grown up together, and maybe that's why this friendship feels so different.

You know the versions of me that most people don't. And I know the versions of you that most people don't. And somehow, after seeing all of it, we still love each other endlessly.

On your birthday, I don't just want to remind you how much I love you. I want to remind you of who you are. You're stronger than you think. You're capable of so much more than you give yourself credit for. You've grown so much since the girl I met in 2020, and I'm genuinely proud of the person you're becoming.

The reminder, in full

“Oi, you're going to be okay.”

Chapter five

One more thing

The hopes, listed for the record

So whenever life gets confusing, whenever you question yourself, whenever you feel like you're falling behind, I hope you remember that I'll always be somewhere in the background reminding you of it.

And yes, I will probably still be giving you terrible advice, judging your boy choices, forcing you to come for coffee, convincing you to make questionable plans and laughing at you when you do something stupid. Some things will never change.

I hope life gives you everything you've ever prayed for. I hope you fall in love with someone who actually deserves you. I hope you build the career you've dreamed about. I hope you travel everywhere you want to go. I hope you become the happiest version of yourself.

And when all of that happens, I hope I'm still there somewhere beside you, watching it happen and saying: “Remember when we had absolutely no idea what we were doing?”

Chapter six · the last one

We never become strangers

Signed off by the editor

Maybe Australia will happen. Maybe our plans will change. Maybe life will take us to completely different places. Maybe we'll be busy, far away, married, working, growing, and doing a hundred different things.

But I hope no matter how much life changes, we never become strangers. Because you aren't just my best friend anymore. You're a part of my life story. A part of my growing-up years. A part of so many of my favourite memories. And honestly, one of the best things that ever came out of college.

So here's to another year of you being your chaotic, beautiful, stubborn, loving self. Here's to more birthdays, more cafés, more trips, more gossip, more career talks, more questionable decisions, more prayers, more growing, and hopefully fewer terrible boys.

Happy birthday to my person, my Puntu, my forever partner in stupidity. I love you endlessly. And if you ever forget how much you mean to me, come back and read this.

“Now please grow up a little. But not too much. I still need my stupid best friend.”

Palbika · Kathmandu
